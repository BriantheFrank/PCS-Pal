PCS Checklist

Use this checklist to keep track of what needs to happen before, during, and after your PCS. If this is your first move, start here.

Best for first-time movers, families moving with kids, and anyone trying to stay organized before pack-out and arrival.

Start by: adding your orders date, report date, and the documents you know you will need to keep with you.

As soon as you get orders

Start fast on paperwork, housing, and family planning so problems do not pile up later.

Review orders carefully and confirm report date. Start a move folder or binder for all important paperwork, decide what must stay with you during travel, and begin housing research early, especially if availability may be tight.

Start school and childcare planning if applicable, review expected travel timeline and likely lodging needs, discuss whether any part of the move will be personally handled, and confirm whether any family medical or EFMP needs may require extra coordination.

  • Common mistake: Waiting too long to gather records and housing information can create avoidable stress later.

30–45 days before the move

Shift from planning to action so you control what travels with you and what gets packed.

Confirm moving dates and shipment expectations. Start sorting what to keep, donate, store, or discard. Create a hand-carry document list and refill prescriptions to protect medical continuity.

Request school records or other child-related paperwork, review pet requirements and lodging rules, check spouse employment or licensing issues if moving to a new state, and start utility, lease, or housing transition planning.

  • Common mistake: Assuming all records can be easily retrieved later. Keep copies of the most important documents with you.

2 weeks before pack-out

Protect essentials now so they do not disappear into household goods.

Separate documents, valuables, medications, and daily essentials from household goods. Confirm temporary lodging and travel stops. Set aside items needed immediately after arrival.

Photograph valuable or fragile items before pack-out, label priority boxes, create an open-first list, confirm school, childcare, and medical transition details, and prepare children and pets for travel-day disruptions as much as possible.

  • Common mistake: Letting essential items get packed because they were not clearly separated ahead of time.

Pack-out week

Treat this week like operations: separate critical items and document what matters.

Keep orders, IDs, records, medications, chargers, and valuables with you. Walk through the home and make sure restricted or do-not-pack items stay separate.

Check box labels for clarity and consistency, keep notes on high-value or fragile items, save receipts and documents that may matter later, and double-check lodging, route timing, and arrival plans.

  • Do not pack: anything you will need during travel, right after arrival, or to resolve a problem quickly.

Travel days

Plan for delays and fatigue. Your goal is a smooth first night, not a perfect schedule.

Keep all critical documents accessible, not buried in luggage. Confirm lodging reservations before arrival when possible and keep medications, snacks, chargers, and child or pet essentials easy to reach.

Track receipts if they may matter later, reconfirm next-day route or arrival timing as needed, and keep one small bag or bin with first-night essentials.

  • Travel tip: Assume you may arrive tired, late, or off schedule. Plan for a smoother first night, not a perfect one.
  • Common mistake: Assuming you can grab important documents quickly if they are buried in luggage.

First week after arrival

Focus on reporting, housing, health, and immediate family function before anything else.

Confirm check-in requirements and key reporting timelines. Verify lodging and housing next steps. Locate the nearest pharmacy, grocery store, and urgent-care or medical support.

Review expected household goods delivery timeline, begin school or childcare follow-up, update critical family records that must change after the move, and unpack essential items first while documenting claim-sensitive items.

  • Common mistake: Trying to do everything immediately. Focus first on housing, reporting, health, and basic family functionality.

After delivery

Document issues while details are fresh so claims and follow-up are easier.

Check items against your inventory or box list, note missing or damaged items as soon as possible, photograph any damage clearly, and keep claim-related paperwork in one place.

Update your inventory notes as you unpack and review what worked and what you would do differently next time.

  • Claims reminder: Good documentation matters. Photos, notes, and clear labels can make follow-up easier.
  • Common mistake: Not documenting damage or missing items while details are still fresh.

If losing it would create a serious problem during travel or arrival, do not put it on the truck. Carry it with you.

First 72 hours after arrival

The first few days after arrival can feel scattered. Focus on the basics first: where you are sleeping, where you need to report, what your family needs immediately, and what can wait a few days.

Family-focused planning guides

Use these quick guides for high-impact family issues that can disrupt PCS timelines.

If you are moving with school-age children, start gathering school records early. Registration requirements vary by district and installation. Keep report cards, immunization records, enrollment forms, and any special education paperwork with you instead of packing them.

Childcare can become a problem quickly during a PCS. If you expect to need care soon after arrival, research options early and get on waitlists where possible. Have a backup plan in case care is not available right away.

If a family member has ongoing medical needs, refill prescriptions before travel and keep care plans, referral paperwork, and provider contact information with you. Do not pack anything you may need during travel or shortly after arrival.

EFMP planning: If a family member has specialized medical or educational needs, start coordination early and keep all related records with you. Delays in documentation can slow access to services after arrival.

If you work in a licensed profession, check early whether your new state requires a transfer or temporary license. That process can take time and may affect how quickly you can return to work.

If you are moving with pets, confirm lodging rules early and keep vaccination records, medications, and travel supplies with you. Pet-related surprises often happen late in the process, especially with hotels and rental restrictions.

Do not assume housing will be ready when you arrive. Build a backup plan for temporary lodging, and understand your likely commute, school options, and utility setup timeline before signing a lease.

If an item is damaged or missing, good documentation makes a big difference. Photograph valuable items before pack-out, keep notes on condition, and review shipments carefully at delivery before signing away issues.

How to organize your PCS paperwork

PCS moves create a lot of paperwork. Keeping it organized in one place makes it easier to solve problems quickly if something changes during travel, check-in, housing, or delivery.

Use a binder, accordion folder, or digital folder structure that you can access quickly while traveling.
